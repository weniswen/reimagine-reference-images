## Reference-style authority

Technical cleanliness must not erase intentional style. Preserve reference-led Y2K color, early-digital texture, retro-game plastic, direct flash, chromatic lighting, camp, punk roughness, toy-like futurism, deliberate awkwardness, or controlled visual chaos when it is part of the style fingerprint.

Do not automatically convert distinctive references into premium editorial photography, serene human-interest portraiture, mature sophistication, clean minimalism, or neutral daylight. Those treatments are valid only when supported by the reference or user.

## Desired look

Unless the reference intentionally calls for a different treatment, create images that are:

- clean, bright, clear, and naturally exposed;
- rich in meaningful layers without decorative overload;
- materially believable and free of waxy or plastic surfaces;
- colorful in a controlled, natural system;
- visually confident, with a clear focal hierarchy;
- aesthetically appealing on the reference's own terms, with its luminosity, intimacy, charm, tactile quality, or scenic openness translated rather than discarded;
- free of generic AI aesthetics.

## Feedback persistence and cross-output variation

- Treat direct user criticism as binding for subsequent outputs in the conversation.
- Treat a proposed experiment as provisional. Do not promote it to a standing visual rule until the user accepts it; delete it from later prompts when the user dislikes the result.
- Check recent accepted and rejected images for repeated dominant hue, exposure, setting, light recipe, face type, and finish.
- Do not let one visually strong trait become a default template. In particular, avoid turning every sky, shadow, garment, surface, and reflection blue merely because one reference contained blue.
- If the user rejects a result, preserve only the still-valued traits and rebuild the root cause. Prefer the original reference over the rejected generation for the next reference input.
- A corrective generation must look materially different on the criticized axis. A verbal promise in the prompt is not enough.

## Cleanliness without flattening

Cleanliness means control of accidental noise, dirt, compression, pointless repetition, and incoherent micro-detail. It does not mean mandatory minimalism, textureless surfaces, simplified geometry, or large flat-color CGI.

- Retain medium-specific texture, organic irregularity, depth cues, and local material variation where they create beauty or plausibility.
- Use large readable planes as compositional breathing room, not as a command to turn hills, faces, fabric, fur, vegetation, or water into blocky primitives.
- Do not default to low-poly, clay-like, toy-rendered, spherical, overly smooth, or plasticized forms unless the reference or user requests them.
- If a cleanup experiment makes the result visibly flatter, cheaper, more artificial, or stylistically less appealing, remove that experiment rather than weakening it into a permanent rule.

## Aesthetic appeal and novelty

Identify the reference's **appeal ledger** before changing the world: exposure and luminosity, emotional intimacy, subject scale, facial charm or anonymity, material softness, palette elegance, tactile medium, visual breathing room, and any playful or scenic payoff.

- Preserve an equivalent payoff in the new environment; originality should widen the idea, not erase why the source was attractive.
- Reject novelty created mainly through dark industrial clutter, abrasive surfaces, oppressive machinery, tiny subjects lost in purposeless empty space, muddy exposure, or a giant generic device unless reference-led.
- Keep the intended focal hierarchy readable. In environment-led scenes the environment is the primary subject; small human figures can carry a story without becoming large or facially identifiable.
- For portraits, avoid the two easy failures: a beautiful but generic model in a changed location, and a distinctive mechanism whose harsh staging makes the person or image unattractive.
- Judge the actual result at full view and thumbnail scale. `Interesting but ugly` and `beautiful but normal` both require redesign.

## Hard dislikes

Avoid by default:

- visible luminance or chroma noise, film grain, speckles, compression artifacts;
- oversharpening, crunchy microtexture, artificial clarity, HDR, halos, bloom, glow;
- waxy skin, plastic fur, glossy CGI rock, vegetation, paper, fabric, or ceramics;
- generic or incoherent cyberpunk, futuristic neon, and technological blue-purple-green mixing when unsupported by the reference;
- yellow cast, golden veil, warm haze, foggy cream filter, or muddy brown grading;
- dark blocked foregrounds paired with bright skies;
- huge blank skies, empty ground, or small figures lost in purposeless emptiness (not deliberate environment-led scale);
- repeated decorative elements, perfect duplication, dense particles, or confetti detail;
- every object emitting light or every surface competing for attention.
- repeated near-monochrome blue treatment across unrelated outputs;
- underexposed faces, blocked clothing, or a bright environment that leaves the subject muddy;
- gray or khaki veils that flatten local color and depth;
- idealized synthetic faces with poreless skin, beauty-filter symmetry, glossy lips, enlarged eyes, pinched noses, or generic influencer features;
- technically realistic but visually unintentional photography that feels like a casual phone snapshot.

Natural blue, green, or purple may appear. Coherent synthetic neon mixing and technological glow may also appear when required by a Y2K, retro-futurist, game-like, or science-fiction style fingerprint. Reject only generic, muddy, or incoherent use.

## Scene design before negative prompting

Do not rely on "no noise" alone. Remove likely noise sources from the concept:

- avoid combining gravel, dense leaves, seaweed, spray, particles, and rough rock across the whole frame;
- avoid deep caves, dark forests, and other high-dynamic-range scenes unless bright exposure is essential to the request;
- use large readable planes as buffers: calm water, sand, walls, sky, broad fabric, or architectural surfaces;
- keep those planes materially and spatially convincing rather than uniformly smooth or low-poly;
- keep fine texture local to focal materials;
- create richness through layers, activity, scale, and shape—not countless small objects.

## Exposure and color

- Prefer reference-consistent light. Use neutral daylight only when the source or concept supports it; retain direct flash, colored studio light, screen light, or synthetic futuristic light when style-defining.
- Keep whites neutral and shadows open/readable.
- Use one main palette plus a few separated accents.
- Avoid default golden hour; use it only when the reference or user requires it.
- Preserve color contrast without resorting to teal-orange or neon complementary grading.
- Distribute color by material and depth rather than washing the entire frame with one hue.
- Judge dominant color across the complete image, not only the named palette. If sky, shadows, clothing, and architecture all converge on one hue unintentionally, redesign the scene.
- Avoid using desaturation as a shortcut to realism. Natural images still need separated greens, skin tones, sky colors, and material neutrals.

## Photographic authorship

For photographic outputs, require at least one intentional visual decision beyond accurate depiction:

- a decisive or directional light event;
- a strong foreground anchor or leading path;
- an expressive camera-to-subject relation;
- controlled motion against a stable subject;
- layered atmospheric or ecological depth;
- a meaningful reflection, reveal, scale contrast, or spatial compression.

Do not add all of these at once. Use the smallest set that gives the image a clear point of view. Reject flat eye-level documentation, weak central framing, and uniform overcast light when they make the result feel accidental. Do not compensate with HDR, excessive saturation, fake sun rays, or stock-photo gloss.

### Story and dreamlike atmosphere

A concept title or explanation is not evidence of a story. Require a visible relationship, trace, tension, or consequence that reads without the caption. Prefer environmental storytelling and simple natural actions over a prominently posed performer.

When dreamlike beauty is requested, design coherent light, depth, material, scale, and visual rhythm. Do not assume that brighter exposure, smooth white architecture, large glowing animals, and a visitor pointing upward produce beauty or narrative. This aquarium treatment was rejected as generic. The later large foreground dancer also failed: complex limbs and a readable face violated the standing preference. Neither is an accepted template. Retain a praised motif without inheriting rejected staging; do not make aquarium, pastel, or dreamlike styling a universal requirement.

## Portrait face realism

These checks apply to whatever facial detail is naturally visible; they do not authorize increasing face scale or identifiability against the standing preference.

- Preserve human specificity: natural facial proportions, slight asymmetry, believable eyelids and nostrils, real hairline and ears, subtle skin variation, and context-appropriate expression.
- Keep skin texture visible but restrained. Avoid both poreless smoothing and exaggerated pores or blemishes.
- Use lighting that keeps the face readable and dimensional without beauty-filter sculpting. The face should not be the darkest important region in the frame.
- Inspect eyes, mouth, nose, jaw, ears, hairline, hands, and their relationship to camera perspective. Reject a face that is attractive only because it has been generalized into an AI beauty archetype.
- Prefer candid specificity over generic model perfection, while preserving the reference's life stage, presentation, and attitude.

### Intentional face de-emphasis

A realistic person does not require a clearly readable face. Low identifiability and small figure scale are defaults for this skill unless explicitly overridden:

- keep anatomy, skin, hair, light, and occlusion realistic in the visible fragments;
- move expression into posture, action, clothing, gesture, and environmental response;
- use compositionally meaningful side/back angles, scale, hair, reflection, refraction, overlap, or shadow;
- preserve accepted color, motion, composition, and photographic quality through a narrow edit lock;
- avoid accidental creepiness, faceless smoothing, melted features, censor bars, pixelation, arbitrary blur, or a pasted-on covering object.

Do not change demographics merely to make the subject more appealing. `Less clearly shown` and `different-looking person` are separate requests.

## Landscape image strength

- Natural realism does not require a gray, flat, casual snapshot finish.
- Build depth with a foreground entry, midground flow, and background destination when the scene supports them.
- Use a real scenic mechanism such as clearing weather, reflected light, wind, mist, hydrology, or an unusual ecological rhythm when a calm scene needs distinction.
- Keep color vivid enough to separate materials, but let light and composition create impact. Do not solve dullness with blanket warmth, saturation, or default golden hour.
- Reject a landscape that is technically plausible yet lacks a focal hierarchy, directional light, discovery, or an authored spatial rhythm.

## Physical continuity and natural-process logic

Validate dynamic scenes as connected systems, not as attractive isolated regions. Trace:

`driver/source -> direction -> boundary -> transition -> consequence -> dissipation`

- A sharp change between calm and turbulent water, dry and wet material, clear and smoky air, or light and shadow requires a visible cause such as depth, shelter, obstruction, slope, wind, or time.
- For waves, identify the deep-water side, propagation direction, shoaling or reef/sandbar boundary, breaking side, whitewater/surf zone, and downstream calming. Clear reef water is possible; a large breaker immediately adjoining glassy shallow water without foam, turbulence, or current continuity is not convincing.
- For poured liquids, preserve a continuous source, plausible stream thickness, gravity direction, impact splash, pooling, overflow, and drainage. Do not turn clear water into a dense rope of fine foam unless soap, aeration, pressure, or another visible cause supports it.
- For smoke and flame, align flame lean, smoke rise, ember direction, burn marks, and nearby material response with airflow and heat.
- For wind, current, impact, and motion, make hair, fabric, foliage, spray, wakes, suspended debris, and body posture agree on the same force field.
- Surrealism may change one stated law or relationship. Keep all unselected physics coherent so the intended impossibility reads as deliberate rather than accidental.

## Prop and receptacle coherence

- Give every prominent prop a narrative function, a mechanically plausible role, and a compositional reason to occupy its location and scale.
- Reject a container, basin, tray, pedestal, tool, or support added only as a generic solution if its shape, finish, or visual weight weakens the image.
- Integrate functional objects into the scene's material language. When a receptacle is necessary, choose or design one whose geometry, color, surface, and placement reinforce the concept.
- Check access, attachment, capacity, balance, drainage, and contact. A beautiful prop that cannot perform its depicted function still fails.

## Environment-led beauty and visual tension

Environment-led does not mean an environment plus a few token people. A complete picture can be made from light, weather, spatial relationships, object states, traces of events and reflections. Omit humans and animals when their only purpose is to make a scene seem alive. Conversely, an animal interaction central to a comic concept is meaningful rather than filler.

Avoid resolving originality with a dull new subject: evenly arranged tables around a blank center, a pale animal close-up, or a generic empty venue may be different but aesthetically weaker. Build meaningful large/medium/small forms, overlaps, viewpoint, directional light and coherent color contrast. Tension can be compositional or temporal; danger, exaggerated action and a protagonist are unnecessary. Do not add clutter merely to fill space.

Use color to organize attention: preserve separation of materials and a deliberate dominant/accent relationship. “Soft and dreamy” can still have vivid focal color and readable depth. Optical softness should come from plausible focus, motion or reflected light, not a flat pastel wash or haze over the entire image. Change the characteristic hue AND surface response when both are recognition anchors; velvet-to-thin-translucent, matte-to-reflective, or another physically fitting change may work, but never force one material recipe onto every subject.

## Composition density

Use meaningful foreground, midground, and background anchors when the category supports depth. Do not fill space merely to avoid emptiness.

- Landscape: enrich with terrain, hydrology, vegetation, weather, and natural landmarks; do not add people by default.
- Animal: keep one clear animal subject and enough environmental evidence for behavior and physics.
- Portrait/concept: give the person a meaningful relation to the environment; avoid generic standing poses.
- Anonymous or face-de-emphasized portrait: keep the person compositionally expressive through silhouette, action, gesture, clothing, scale, or environmental interaction; do not let reduced facial detail create a weak focal hierarchy.
- Playful/weird portrait: preserve the joke, prop interaction, attitude, era signal, and designed awkwardness; reject generic human-interest treatment even when technically polished.
- Illustration/collage: fill the frame with large and medium shapes, clear hierarchy, and controlled overlaps; avoid dirty texture and pseudo-detail.

## Medium-specific cleanliness

### Photorealistic

- Use natural optics, plausible depth of field, authentic material response, and coherent exposure.
- Avoid stock-photo gloss, game-concept rendering, and hyperreal micro-detail.
- Avoid casual phone-snapshot composition unless the reference or user explicitly asks for it.
- Allow intentional direct flash, compact-camera character, retro-digital color, and synthetic materials when required by the reference fingerprint.

### Flat illustration

- Use crisp matte fills, smooth edges, and deliberate shape language.
- Avoid gradients, grain, paper dirt, glossy 3D shading, and accidental pseudo-text unless requested.

### Collage

- Use clean paper edges and restrained tactile fiber.
- Avoid distressed overlays, stains, halftone noise, and repeated clip-art motifs.

## Text default

Render no typography unless explicitly requested. A reference containing text does not authorize text in the new image. Explicitly prohibit words, letters, numbers, logos, labels, signs, watermarks, and pseudo-text.
