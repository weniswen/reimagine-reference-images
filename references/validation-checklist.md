# Validation checklist

## Contents

- Gate discipline
- Request and category
- Originality distance
- Creative strength, whole-image hierarchy, and desirability
- Physics, props, composition, and aesthetic quality
- Anatomy, feedback regression, and correction

Reject or regenerate the output if any critical item fails.

## Gate discipline

Judge three independent hard gates:

1. **Originality:** distinct dominant recognition anchors, relational signature, and arrangement.
2. **Desirability:** a compelling, reference-faithful whole image that is neither merely normal nor aesthetically weak; thumbnail composition comes first, but any prominent human must also pass an independent subject-design gate.
3. **Execution:** visual quality, physical coherence, feedback compliance, and artifact control.

All three must pass. Do not accept a beautiful output that fails originality, an original output that is ugly or generic, or an appealing concept with weak execution.

## A. Request and category

- [ ] Exact requested aspect ratio is visually correct.
- [ ] Reference is used as inspiration, not inadvertently edited or recreated.
- [ ] Broad category is preserved unless the user requested a change.
- [ ] The reference's creative engine, style era, attitude, and weirdness or playfulness level remain recognizable.
- [ ] Visible human life stage and presentation are preserved unless the user requested a change.
- [ ] Landscape references remain landscape-led and contain no unrequested person.
- [ ] Text, logos, and watermarks are absent unless explicitly requested.

## B. Originality distance

- [ ] A 3–6 trait style fingerprint remains, including the creative mechanism and any central era or subculture signal.
- [ ] A visual-force profile remains—camera attitude, spatial compression, direction, contrast, saturation, scale tension, material response, and campaign attitude—without copying literal content.
- [ ] 4–6 transformation axes changed, with distance appropriate to the reference type.
- [ ] The one to three dominant recognition anchors were identified and broken when they were literal rather than stylistic.
- [ ] Axis count was not used to excuse a surviving face scale, lens relation, signature pose, prop-face pairing, silhouette, or environment enclosure.
- [ ] Literal identity and recognizable arrangement changed without arbitrary demographic drift.
- [ ] Environment skeleton differs when the setting is central to recognition or stronger reconstruction was selected.
- [ ] When the source setting was incidental, multiple setting families—including a cross-environment option—were considered before selection.
- [ ] Pose, silhouette, focal placement, route shape, cluster layout, crop, and palette mapping are not recognizably copied.
- [ ] The relational signature—actor, action, focal object, support surface, surrounding object family, and camera relation—is not recognizably copied through renamed or recolored substitutes.
- [ ] A short first-glance description of the output is materially different from a short first-glance description of the reference.
- [ ] The result has its own narrative or natural mechanism.
- [ ] Age, gender, ethnicity, and attitude were not used as arbitrary originality shortcuts.
- [ ] The same demographic profile has not been repeated across recent outputs without a reference-based reason.

## C. Creative or scenic strength

- [ ] The concept can be summarized in one compelling sentence.
- [ ] The thumbnail works through large and medium shapes, color distribution, depth, and direction before faces or fine detail are inspected.
- [ ] Conceptual images contain contradiction, transformation, conflict, decisive action, direction, or scale tension.
- [ ] The selected concept is not merely the same subject, pose, or behavior moved into a more impressive location.
- [ ] The concept contains an appropriate interaction, consequence, causal event, contradiction, transformation, discovery, directional force, or spatial rule.
- [ ] Calm landscapes contain discovery, flow, layered ecology, or a distinctive natural event.
- [ ] The output is not a literal synonym swap.
- [ ] A playful, weird, Y2K, retro-futurist, game-like, camp, or rebellious reference has not been sanitized into generic premium editorial, mature portraiture, serene human-interest photography, or tasteful minimalism.
- [ ] The result preserves the reference's level of humor, personality, audacity, or designed awkwardness.
- [ ] The focal hierarchy is immediately readable.
- [ ] Each subject has an intentional salience role: hero, participant, secondary anchor, scale cue, or absent.
- [ ] In non-portrait imagery, people do not become the largest, nearest, brightest, or highest-contrast visual mass unless human dominance is reference-critical or explicitly requested.
- [ ] The mechanism, environment, path, material response, or interacting system remains the primary thumbnail read when the user prioritizes the overall image.
- [ ] Removing any person, animal, product, or prop was considered; every retained subject improves the whole image rather than merely filling space or providing scale.
- [ ] Multiple prominent elements share a legible interaction graph: `driver -> affected element -> visible response -> consequence`.
- [ ] No passive bystander is placed beside a giant or hero object without a meaningful, aesthetically strong relationship.
- [ ] Photographic outputs show an intentional point of view through light, framing, motion, depth, reflection, or scale rather than merely resembling a plausible snapshot.
- [ ] Landscapes do not feel gray, flat, or casually documented when the user expects a visually strong reimagining.
- [ ] The reference's real aesthetic payoff—luminosity, intimacy, charm, texture, subject scale, palette, breathing room, or scenic openness—survives in an equivalent form.
- [ ] Novelty does not depend on arbitrary darkness, abrasive industrial clutter, oppressive scale, a tiny isolated subject, or an oversized generic prop.
- [ ] Unusual scale creates function, tension, interaction, and beauty rather than acting as an originality shortcut.
- [ ] The result passes both failure tests: it is neither `interesting but ugly` nor `beautiful but generic`.

## D. Physics and causality

For any dynamic or material interaction, trace:

`support -> force direction -> center of gravity -> muscle/action response -> material deformation -> expression`

- [ ] Weight has a visible support path.
- [ ] Feet, paws, wheels, boats, tools, and objects contact surfaces correctly.
- [ ] Gravity, inertia, wind, current, and tension point in consistent directions.
- [ ] Body posture and center of gravity respond to the situation.
- [ ] Muscles, grip, joint angles, and expression match the exertion or risk.
- [ ] Clothing, straps, ropes, water, sand, hair, and other materials respond to force.
- [ ] Shadows, reflections, wakes, spray, deposits, and contact marks agree with motion and light.
- [ ] A scene-level chain can be traced as `source/driver -> propagation -> boundary/interaction -> transition -> consequence -> dissipation`.
- [ ] Upstream/downstream, shoreward/seaward, windward/leeward, inside/outside, and deep/shallow zones are spatially consistent where relevant.
- [ ] Calm/turbulent, dry/wet, clear/foamy, lit/shadowed, or intact/damaged boundaries have visible causes and believable transition widths.
- [ ] Waves preserve depth-driven shoaling, a coherent breaking direction, whitewater on the downstream/shoreward side, and gradual turbulence decay; clear bottom visibility does not erase surface disturbance.
- [ ] Liquid streams preserve source composition, gravity, continuity, impact, pooling, overflow, and drainage; foam density has a visible cause.
- [ ] Fire, smoke, wind, current, spray, hair, fabric, foliage, and debris agree on force direction and aftermath.
- [ ] Deliberate surrealism is confined to the intended concept; everything else remains coherent.

## E. Prop and compositional function

- [ ] Every prominent prop is necessary or meaningfully expressive rather than generic filler.
- [ ] Every prominent co-subject affects or responds to the same causal or compositional system; adjacency alone is not treated as interaction.
- [ ] Containers, basins, trays, tools, supports, and receptacles suit the concept's material language and do not weaken the composition through awkward shape, finish, scale, or placement.
- [ ] Functional props have plausible capacity, access, attachment, balance, contact, and drainage.
- [ ] Removing or redesigning a visually weak functional object was considered before accepting it.

## F. Composition and density

- [ ] Foreground, midground, and background are meaningful where appropriate.
- [ ] No huge blank region weakens the image.
- [ ] No micro-clutter or repeated filler makes the image noisy.
- [ ] Large and medium shapes organize the frame.
- [ ] Subject scale is intentional, not accidentally tiny or isolated.
- [ ] A secondary or scale-cue subject has not accidentally become a centered face-led portrait because of camera direction, eye contact, or lighting.
- [ ] The composition remains strong when faces and micro-details are unreadable at thumbnail scale.

## G. Exposure and aesthetic profile

- [ ] Exposure supports the reference style and required details remain readable.
- [ ] Color cast is intentional and reference-supported; no accidental yellow veil or foggy cream cast.
- [ ] No visible noise, grain, compression, excessive sharpening, HDR, halo, bloom, or glow.
- [ ] No waxy skin, plastic fur, glossy CGI material, or synthetic AI sheen.
- [ ] Cleanup did not flatten the image into low-poly geometry, blocky large-color shapes, clay-like forms, textureless surfaces, or plastic smoothing.
- [ ] Intentional organic texture and medium character remain without becoming dirty or noisy.
- [ ] Futuristic, Y2K, neon, and technological color are coherent and reference-supported rather than generic or muddy.
- [ ] Colors are controlled, natural or medium-appropriate, and not muddy.
- [ ] No unintended near-monochrome wash covers unrelated materials, especially repeated all-blue treatment across sky, shadows, clothing, and environment.
- [ ] No gray, khaki, or low-contrast veil suppresses material color and depth.
- [ ] Grayness or desaturation has not been used as a shortcut to sophistication when the reference or feedback requires vivid energy.
- [ ] Color energy comes from separated local colors, contrast, and accent placement—not a global saturation boost, warm filter, or monochrome wash.
- [ ] Important faces and subjects are bright and dimensional enough to read; the background is not doing all the visual work.

## H. Human-subject design, anatomy, and object integrity

- [ ] Every hero or active human has a readable silhouette and one intentional dominant body line.
- [ ] The chosen action phase balances force, compression, and extension; it is not an awkward transitional instant, collapsed torso, or accidental squat.
- [ ] Weight-bearing and counterbalancing limbs create a coherent, graceful posture while remaining physically correct.
- [ ] Arms, torso, legs, hands, clothing, and equipment retain useful negative spaces and do not merge into confusing tangencies.
- [ ] Camera angle and foreshortening create energy without awkwardly enlarging extremities, shortening the torso, widening the body, or turning the head into an unintended visual block.
- [ ] Clothing fit, volume, seam direction, color blocking, and material response clarify the pose and reference-led attitude rather than hiding mechanics under generic bulk or costume noise.
- [ ] Each outfit follows one coherent wardrobe plan: reference-aligned attitude/era, silhouette family, layering, fit, material, restrained palette, and integrated gear.
- [ ] Clothing does not rely on random high-saturation separates, mismatched primary colors, generic technical sportswear, cropped performance jackets, featureless bodysuits, or bulky rental-looking protection as style shortcuts.
- [ ] Necessary helmets, harnesses, pads, straps, and fasteners remain visually subordinate and do not erase the garment or body silhouette.
- [ ] The outfit remains aesthetically intentional when judged independently from the face, action, and environment; it does not read as costume, cheap campaign styling, or random AI fashion.
- [ ] The person's light, palette, shadow, motion, wind response, and material treatment belong to the same visual system as the environment.
- [ ] Human appeal does not rely on a gender, age, ethnicity, body-type, hairstyle, sexualization, or generic-model-face substitution.
- [ ] A visually strong face or hairstyle has not been accepted as compensation for a weak silhouette, pose, gesture, garment, or camera relation.

- [ ] Correct number of limbs, hands, paws, fingers, wheels, straps, and supports.
- [ ] Equipment is functional and attached logically.
- [ ] Faces and expressions are intentional and situation-appropriate.
- [ ] Faces retain believable proportions, slight asymmetry, natural skin variation, eyelids, nostrils, ears, hairline, and lip texture rather than an AI beauty archetype.
- [ ] Portrait faces are not poreless, waxy, over-sculpted, generically glamorous, or incongruously perfect.
- [ ] When lower face visibility was requested, the face is materially less prominent or identifiable through a physically motivated method.
- [ ] When the overall image was prioritized, the subject is framed from a suitable side/back angle, reduced in salience, or omitted rather than unnecessarily restyled.
- [ ] Reduced visibility did not rely on an inferred demographic preference, face replacement, censor bar, mosaic pixelation, arbitrary blur, or malformed features.
- [ ] A face-de-emphasized subject remains expressive through pose, gesture, clothing, action, silhouette, or environmental interaction.
- [ ] Repeated objects are not accidentally duplicated.
- [ ] No pseudo-text appears on objects or in the environment.

## I. Feedback regression and recent-output variation

- [ ] Every explicit user criticism from the current conversation has been converted into a visible acceptance criterion.
- [ ] Speculative suggestions were recorded as experiments rather than silently promoted to permanent constraints.
- [ ] Any experiment the user disliked was removed completely from later prompts and outputs.
- [ ] The output materially changes the rejected axis rather than repeating it with different wording.
- [ ] Root-level criticism such as `ugly`, `ordinary`, `disconnected`, `wrong overall`, or `opposite style` triggered a concept/scene/mechanism review rather than cosmetic patching.
- [ ] When the user accepted the design but requested one narrow correction, an edit lock preserved the accepted scene, composition, palette, lighting, realism, and physics unless a local change was necessary.
- [ ] `Not my type` or similar appearance feedback was interpreted as a visibility request unless the user explicitly requested a different appearance.
- [ ] Recent outputs were checked for repeated dominant hue, exposure, setting family, camera relation, face archetype, and finish.
- [ ] No rejected blue-heavy, dark, gray, plastic-faced, generic, or casual-snapshot treatment has silently returned.
- [ ] No rejected root concept was cosmetically revived through recoloring, extra particles, new clothing, a replacement face, or a giant substitute object.
- [ ] The next generation used the original reference rather than a rejected output unless the user explicitly requested an edit of that output.

## Correction rule

When a check fails:

1. Name the single highest-impact failure.
2. Identify its root level: concept, recognition-anchor bundle, relational similarity, physical topology, interaction graph, prop design, scene design, palette system, exposure, camera relation, subject salience, human-subject design, face rendering, or finish.
3. Revise that root dimension while restating category, ratio, originality, desirability, no-text, physics, feedback, and aesthetic invariants.
4. If the criticism is root-level or the same failure has already occurred, change the scene, mechanism, or medium strategy rather than adding stronger negative adjectives, recoloring, particles, clothing changes, or a giant replacement object.
5. Generate again from the original reference by default.
6. Re-run the entire checklist; do not assume other constraints remained stable.

Reject technically polished but stylistically generic outputs. Do not report success merely because the prompt contained the right words. Validate the actual image against both originality and reference appeal.
